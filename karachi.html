Karachi – City of Lights

Pakistan's largest city — a dynamic metropolis where the Arabian Sea meets urban energy.

About Karachi

Karachi is the largest city in Pakistan and one of the most populous cities in the world, with an estimated population of over 16 million people. Situated on the coast of the Arabian Sea in Sindh province, it is the country's financial capital and its primary seaport, handling the vast majority of Pakistan's international trade. The city earned the title "City of Lights" for its vibrant nightlife, illuminated skyline, and round-the-clock urban activity.

Karachi is a city of extraordinary diversity. Home to people from virtually every ethnic, linguistic, and religious background in Pakistan, it is a melting pot of cultures, languages, and cuisines. The city's seafood, street food, and restaurant scene is legendary — from the fresh fish at Boat Basin to the biryani of Tariq Road. For visitors, Karachi offers an intriguing mix of colonial-era architecture, Mughal-influenced monuments, modern art galleries, and a stunning coastline stretching for over 70 kilometres along the Arabian Sea.

Best Time to Visit

November to February — Karachi's winters are mild and pleasant. Summers are humid and hot, though sea breezes moderate the heat along the coast.

Clifton Beach

Clifton Beach is Karachi's most famous and most visited public beach, stretching along the Arabian Sea in the southern part of the city. It is accessible to residents and visitors of all backgrounds and comes alive especially in the evenings and on weekends, when families, food vendors, camel riders, and musicians converge along the sandy shoreline. The beach is named after the upscale Clifton neighbourhood that surrounds it — one of Karachi's most affluent areas.

Along the beach promenade, visitors will find a fairground with rides, a variety of street food stalls selling everything from fresh sugarcane juice to BBQ corn and seafood snacks, and vendors offering camel and horse rides. Just steps from the beach is the famous Do Darya ("Two Rivers") restaurant strip, which offers fresh seafood with views of the Arabian Sea. Nearby Sea View offers similar seaside experiences with a slightly calmer atmosphere, popular with joggers and families seeking an evening stroll.

Mohatta Palace

Mohatta Palace is one of the most architecturally stunning buildings in Karachi, built in 1927 by Shivratan Chandraratan Mohatta, a wealthy Marwari Hindu merchant who used it as a summer home. The palace is constructed from yellow Jodhpur sandstone and pink stone from Rajputana, giving it a warm, distinctive hue unlike any other building in the city. Its architecture blends Rajputana, Mughal, and European colonial styles, featuring ornate arches, domes, turrets, and intricate stone lattice work.

After Partition in 1947, the palace became the residence of Fatima Jinnah, the sister of Pakistan's founder Muhammad Ali Jinnah, and she lived there until her death in 1967. It was subsequently acquired by the government and converted into the Mohatta Palace Museum, which opened to the public in 1999. The museum houses a permanent collection of Pakistani art, including paintings, sculpture, decorative arts, and historical artefacts. It also hosts rotating exhibitions of contemporary Pakistani artists. The palace's gardens are beautifully maintained and are a popular spot for photography.

Pakistan Maritime Museum

The Pakistan Maritime Museum, established in 1988 and located in the PNS Karsaz naval area of Karachi, is a comprehensive museum dedicated to Pakistan's naval history and maritime heritage. The museum is spread across a large outdoor and indoor complex and contains one of the most unique collections of military hardware in South Asia — including a full-size decommissioned submarine, PNS Hangor, which became famous for sinking the Indian frigate INS Khukri during the 1971 war, the only submarine kill in Asian waters since World War II.

Beyond the submarine, the museum's outdoor area features a decommissioned destroyer, naval aircraft, anti-aircraft guns, torpedoes, and various other naval vessels and equipment. The indoor museum contains detailed exhibits on the history of the Pakistan Navy from its founding in 1947, navigational instruments, scale models of ships and submarines, maritime maps, and artefacts related to Pakistan's role in Indian Ocean trade and defence. The museum is particularly popular with families, students, and military history enthusiasts.
