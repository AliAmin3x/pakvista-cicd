Lahore – City of Gardens

The cultural heart of Pakistan, where Mughal grandeur meets modern energy.

About Lahore

Lahore is Pakistan's second-largest city and the capital of Punjab province. Known as the cultural capital of the country, it has been a centre of art, literature, music, and cuisine for centuries. The city flourished under Mughal rule and its architectural legacy — mosques, forts, and gardens — continues to draw visitors from across the globe.

With a population of over 13 million, Lahore is a city that never sleeps. The narrow lanes of the Walled City buzz with spice merchants, traditional craftsmen, and street food vendors, while modern Lahore offers wide boulevards, universities, and a thriving arts scene. The city earned its nickname "City of Gardens" from its many Mughal-era parks, which remain green oases in the urban landscape.

Best Time to Visit

October to March — mild temperatures make sightseeing comfortable. Summers can be extremely hot (40°C+).

Badshahi Mosque

Built by Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb in 1673, the Badshahi Mosque is one of the most iconic landmarks in Pakistan and one of the largest mosques in the world. The mosque can accommodate up to 100,000 worshippers in its vast courtyard, which is paved with red Makrana marble. Its four minarets soar 53 metres into the sky, flanking a central dome of pure white marble.

The mosque's interior is adorned with intricate frescoes, marble inlay work, and calligraphy in the Thuluth script. Facing the mosque is the Hazuri Bagh garden and the Roshnai Gate — the original lit gateway into the Walled City. The mosque is a UNESCO World Heritage nominated site and remains an active place of worship visited by hundreds of thousands each year.

Lahore Fort (Shahi Qila)

The Lahore Fort, locally known as Shahi Qila (Royal Fort), is a citadel that spans over 20 hectares and contains 21 notable monuments. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981, jointly with the Shalimar Gardens. The fort has been rebuilt and expanded by successive rulers — from the early Mughal emperors Akbar and Jahangir to Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb.

Among its highlights are the Sheesh Mahal (Palace of Mirrors), whose walls and ceilings are encrusted with thousands of tiny mirrors and glass pieces; the Naulakha Pavilion, built of pure white marble inlaid with precious and semi-precious stones; and the Diwan-e-Aam (Hall of Public Audience), where the Mughal emperor would receive petitions from commoners. The fort's Picture Wall is a 45-metre-long mosaic of glazed tile depicting royal scenes.

Shalimar Gardens

The Shalimar Gardens were commissioned by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan in 1641 and completed in 1642. Spread over 16 hectares, the gardens are laid out on three descending terraces and contain 410 fountains fed by a historic canal system that still functions today. The gardens were designed as a royal pleasure garden — a paradise on earth — following the Persian charbagh (four-garden) style.

The highest terrace, called Farah Bakhsh (Bestower of Pleasure), contains the emperor's private pavilion. The middle terrace, Faiz Bakhsh (Bestower of Goodness), is the largest and contains the main cascade waterfall. The lowest terrace was open to the public during the Mughal era. Alongside Lahore Fort, the Shalimar Gardens are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and remain one of the finest surviving examples of Mughal landscape architecture.
